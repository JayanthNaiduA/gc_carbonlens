1. Executive Direction
Recommended development direction: inside-out, core-first, using a thin vertical slice.
Do not spend the first days building every screen, store page, community wrapper or report. The judging model gives 40% to technical merit and 30% to problem alignment. A small but real AI + Google Cloud + sustainability workflow is stronger than a visually broad prototype whose core calculations and AI are mocked.
First prove the scientific/technical core. A product or receipt is converted into structured evidence; CarbonLens calculates a reproducible estimate/score and records confidence.
Then prove the AI value. Gemini explains the trusted result and generates a practical greener alternative. It does not invent the carbon value.
Then prove trust and reward. Verification/duplicate checks gate Green Points so the prototype shows responsible behavior design rather than simple gamification.
Then expand the reward foundation. Reuse the same Trusted Green Event contract for Green-Move, Grow-Green and Recycle-Red once Go-Green-Product works.
Then wrap it in experience. Dashboard, reports, Green Store sample cards, community and Earth Pulse come after the verified action loop is stable.
Figure 1 - Core-first implementation order for the JAPAC prototype
1.1 The prototype story that must work
1. John signs in and accepts only the permissions needed for the selected feature.
2. John scans a product (plastic water bottle) or chooses a provided sample image.
3. Cloud Vision/OCR and product normalization produce a candidate product/category and confidence.
4. The Carbon Engine retrieves a versioned factor/category record and calculates the carbon estimate and Sustainability Score.
5. Gemini receives the structured result and explains why the item scores as it does; it proposes a reusable alternative.
6. A trusted green action or purchase produces a verification decision and eligible Green Points.
7. The dashboard/report updates with carbon, avoided emissions, score and points kept as separate metrics.
8. Optional: Green Move demonstrates low-carbon transport using Health Connect/route evidence.

5.3 Data architecture
Figure 4 - Operational, analytical and AI data separation
Firestore = operational truth. Users, consents, scan jobs/results, reward wallet/ledger, recommendations, sample store/order state.
BigQuery = analytical/scientific truth. Emission factors, product/category sustainability data, green events, daily summaries, reporting aggregates.
Cloud Storage = temporary evidence. Raw product/receipt images have lifecycle deletion; persistent records keep structured facts/hashes where possible.
Gemini context = derived, not authoritative. Only structured, consent-approved facts are sent; generated prose is stored separately from scientific calculations.
5.4 Carbon accounting rules
Carbon-linked action:activity_quantity x emission_factor = estimated_CO2eavoided_CO2e = max(0, baseline_CO2e - actual_CO2e)Universal reward policy:Reward Points = Base Action Points x Impact Factor x Verification Factor x Confidence Factor x Persistence Factor x Anti-Abuse AdjustmentImpact Factor is segment-specific: product improvement delta, avoided CO2e, verified tree survival/persistence, or verified e-waste diversion. Green Points are loyalty/reward units; they are not tradable carbon credits.
Carbon estimate, Sustainability Score, avoided CO2e, confidence and Green Points are separate fields and must never be collapsed into one score.
Product lifecycle boundary may include raw material, manufacturing, packaging, transport, use and end-of-life where supported; otherwise use category estimate + lower confidence.
Recreational walking/running does not automatically become avoided emissions; the app needs a credible transport baseline/counterfactual.
Every factor used by the Carbon Engine must carry a factor_set_version and provenance/source field.
Not every reward action should be converted into a carbon-credit claim. Product replacement may use sustainability improvement, Green-Move may use avoided CO2e, Grow-Green uses verified planting/survival/persistence, and Recycle-Red uses verified hazardous-waste diversion. Carbon estimates remain separate where scientifically supportable.

5.6 Four-Segment Rewarding Architecture - Foundational Design
The reward system is a core domain architecture, not a UI gamification layer. Every eligible action is converted into a canonical Trusted Green Event with evidence, an action-specific impact metric, verification level, confidence, fraud signals, policy version and reward state. The Reward Policy Engine issues value only from that server-side event.

5.6.1 Go-Green-Product
Intent: reward a better replacement decision, not higher spending. A wooden/bamboo toothbrush replacing a disposable plastic toothbrush is eligible only if CarbonLens can establish a comparable category and a meaningful sustainability improvement.
The old/conventional product may be identified from prior history, scan, receipt or user-declared baseline. A self-declared baseline lowers verification/confidence.
The new product must be resolved to a product/category record with Sustainability Score dimensions and confidence. Reward is based on improvement relative to the category/baseline, never purchase price.
Prevent repeat-crediting by product/receipt fingerprint, replacement cooldown, per-category caps and event idempotency. Consumables may use a policy-defined cadence; durable products should not be rewarded repeatedly for the same replacement.
5.6.2 Green-Move
Intent: reward a verified transport substitution. Exercise alone is not sufficient; the user must establish that walking, cycling or another low-carbon mode replaced a plausible motorized trip.
Baseline examples: home-to-office normally by petrol car, motorcycle or taxi. Baseline mode and approximate distance are captured during onboarding/commute setup and can be periodically reconfirmed.
Health Connect provides activity/session evidence; Google Maps Platform may support distance/route checks. Exact route storage should be minimized and consent-controlled.
Fraud checks include impossible speed, overlapping activities, duplicate timestamps, repeated replayed tracks and implausible start/end relationship. Rewards are capped per trip/day.
5.6.3 Grow-Green
Intent: reward planting plus survival, not merely uploading a planting photo. CarbonLens creates a Green Plot that is rendered inside the CarbonLens experience on Google Maps; the authoritative project record remains in CarbonLens.
Initial registration: geo-tagged plot/point, planting date, claimed tree count, species when known, planting photos and consent. Store precise location only as needed and restrict access for private land.
First check: location/time/photo evidence and duplicate-image checks. Gemini/Vision may assist with visual consistency or approximate vegetation/tree presence, but must not be treated as an exact tree counter without validation.
Second check: periodic owner check-ins, for example day 30, day 90, day 180 and day 365. Each check-in must occur near the registered plot and include fresh evidence.
Third check: authenticated CarbonLens visitors/community members can confirm the plot. Visitor confirmations are weighted by account trust, uniqueness, proximity and timing; collusion/repeated mutual confirmations are fraud signals.
Earth Engine can add an independent area-level signal using vegetation/land-cover time series. Sentinel-2 imagery includes 10 m bands and is suitable for vegetation state/change at plot scale, but it cannot reliably prove survival of an individual young sapling. Therefore Earth Engine is supporting evidence, not the only verifier.
Reward value: default to Green Points. A partner-funded campaign may define a monetary-equivalent sponsorship such as "up to $20 per verified surviving tree," but that amount is a CarbonLens/partner policy, not a Google Maps payment and must not be hard-coded as a universal entitlement.

5.6.4 Recycle-Red
Intent: reward safe diversion of environmentally hazardous electronics/e-waste to a verified disposal or recycling channel. The app should verify the hand-off, not reward a photograph of waste alone.
Before disposal: capture item category/photo and optional device identifiers only if privacy and policy allow. Do not store unnecessary serial numbers.
At disposal: user submits recycler receipt/certificate. Document AI extracts recycler name, date, document number, amount/weight and line items when present.
CarbonLens checks the recycler against a curated verified-recycler registry, evaluates location/date plausibility, creates a normalized certificate fingerprint and blocks duplicate claims across accounts.
Reward is driven by verified waste category/weight and policy-defined hazard/resource-recovery value. Any carbon benefit is shown separately and only when a sourced factor exists.
5.6.5 Common Reward Policy, State Machine and Anti-Gaming
Use one policy engine across all four segments so the product can evolve without embedding reward logic in Flutter or Gemini.
Universal policy formula (conceptual): Reward Points = Base Action Points x Impact Factor x Verification Factor x Confidence Factor x Persistence Factor x Anti-Abuse Adjustment. Each segment defines its own Impact Factor and caps. For carbon-linked actions, avoided CO2e may be one input; it is not mandatory for every segment.
State
Meaning
Allowed transition
PENDING
Evidence received; checks not complete.
-> PROVISIONAL, VESTED or REJECTED
PROVISIONAL
Initial verification passed but persistence/additional evidence is required.
-> VESTED, REJECTED or REVOKED
VESTED
Reward permanently earned under the current policy.
Normally terminal; admin reversal only through audited compensating ledger entry.
REJECTED
Evidence insufficient, inconsistent or policy-ineligible.
May be resubmitted as a new event if policy allows.
REVOKED
Previously provisional value removed because fraud/conflicting evidence was confirmed.
Terminal; preserve audit reason.
Every reward calculation records policy_version, evidence_refs, verification_level, confidence, factors, caps applied and reason codes.
Ledger is append-only. Never edit a historical issued entry; use a compensating/reversal entry with audit reason.
Only backend service identities may create ledger entries. Flutter clients, Gemini and analytics jobs cannot directly change balances.
Cross-account duplicate evidence, suspicious image reuse, implausible location/time, excessive frequency, collusive confirmations and repeated certificates feed the fraud/risk score.

10. Detailed Implementation Sequence
10.1 Step 1 - Establish contracts before screens
Define the canonical ScanResult, CarbonResult, SustainabilityScore, VerificationDecision, RewardLedgerEntry and Recommendation structures. This prevents Flutter, Gemini and BigQuery from using incompatible meanings.
10.2 Step 2 - Build the shell and identity boundary
Flutter creates login, consent and dashboard routes. Firebase Authentication owns credentials. App Check token accompanies sensitive calls. The backend derives uid from verified token, never from a client-supplied uid.
10.3 Step 3 - Deploy the API immediately
A minimal Cloud Run service exposes /health, /version and authenticated /v1/me. Enable structured logging and request IDs from day one so later errors are diagnosable.
10.4 Step 4 - Make the Carbon Engine deterministic
Start with 8-10 product/category examples and a small, transparent factor dataset. Implement calculations and confidence before broad product recognition. Every result includes factor_set_version and method.
10.5 Step 5 - Add Gemini as an explanation layer
Gemini receives the result, evidence and candidate alternatives. Force structured JSON output. Validate before returning. The AI may explain and recommend; it may not write reward ledger entries or invent factors.
10.6 Step 6 - Replace seeded evidence with real image input
Upload image to Cloud Storage, run Vision/OCR or Document AI, normalize to product/category, then feed exactly the same Carbon Engine contract used by the Oct 11 seeded flow. This makes Oct 12 an input-adapter change rather than a redesign.
10.7 Step 7 - Add trust and reward gating
Normalize every eligible action into a canonical green_event with action_type = GO_GREEN_PRODUCT | GREEN_MOVE | GROW_GREEN | RECYCLE_RED. Assign V0-V3 verification, confidence, fraud flags and action-specific impact. Reward Policy Engine applies versioned base/impact/verification/confidence/persistence rules and writes an append-only ledger using unique event_id. Grow-Green supports provisional rewards until persistence checkpoints are met.
10.8 Step 8 - Complete the behavior loop
Dashboard and reports read daily summaries/operational state, show what changed and suggest one next action. This closes Observe → Understand → Change → Verify → Reward → Repeat.
10.9 Step 9 - Expand the same reward foundation to the remaining three segments
After Go-Green-Product is stable, reuse the same Trusted Green Event and Reward Policy contracts for Green-Move, Grow-Green and Recycle-Red. Green-Move requires a commute substitution baseline; Grow-Green requires plot/check-in persistence; Recycle-Red requires verified recycler evidence. Keep these as thin vertical slices rather than separate mini-apps.
10.10 Step 10 - Freeze, harden and tell the story
After Oct 16, prioritize reliability, permissions, logs, screenshots, README, architecture and demo. Do not trade a stable public link for one more feature.

MVP control

User-scoped Firestore rules and backend IAM. Runtime service accounts receive least privilege.

Verified recycler registry + document fingerprint + cross-account duplicate detection + date/location plausibility.
Partner-funded reward value
Monetary-equivalent sponsor campaigns are separate from Green Points, versioned, budget-capped and legally reviewed. Never present Google Maps/Earth Engine as the payer.
